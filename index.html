As a Jane Coffin Childs postdoctoral fellow (2024-27) and Charles H. Revson Senior Biomedical Science fellow (2026-28) in the Falkner Lab lab at Princeton University, I investigate how sex-hormones that fluctuate over hours to days reshape neural computations that guide moment-to-moment decisions. By combining systems neuroscience and lonitudinal Neuropixels recordings with machine-learning approaches for quantifying social behavior and tools for hormonal state measurement and manipulation, I bring a systems and computational lens to neuroendocrinology.

Previously, as a Herchel Smith PhD Fellow, in the Polley Lab at Harvard University, I studied how interconnected neural circuits transform sensation into perception. I investigated how sensory representations are shaped by internal state, expectations, and compensatory plasticity following sensory loss. Using multi-region electrophysiology across auditory and limbic circuits, projection-specific optogenetic tagging, optical measurements of neuromodulator release, I identified transformations in neural coding along the auditory hierarchy and in top-down pathways linking auditory cortex with limbic centers such as the amygdala. I brought this systems neuroscience toolkit to the study of complex, naturalistic social behaviors and brain-body physiology during my postdoctoral work.

My interdisciplinary trajectory into neuroscience began during my undergraduate studies at Indian Institute of Technology (IIT) Madras in India where I majored in Electrical Engineering, minored in Biomedical Engineering and I was drawn to applying quantitative approaches to biological questions.

The overarching goal for my future independent lab is to address how sex-hormones remodel brain-wide networks across the lifespan to prepare the brain for shifting behavioral demands. My long-term vision is to establish a unifying framework of hormonal action on the brain during major life-stage transitions including pregnancy, post-partum and peri-menopause, with the ultimate goal of informing interventions when adaptive plasticity becomes maladaptive such as in post-partum depression.

Alongside science, feminism and equity have been enduring passions of mine, shaped by my experiences growing up as a woman in India. I care deeply about enhancing visibility of women neuroscientists and fostering an inclusive scientific community, and it is especially meaningful to bring these parallel interests together in my research on women's mental health. Drawing on my own interdisciplinary training across engineering and neuroscience, I hope to mentor trainees with diverse backgrounds and perspectives and contribute to a collaborative community that shapes both the science and the next generation of scientists.

I grew up in Chennai, India where we only have three seasons - hot, hotter and hottest, so I naturally fell in love with winter when I first moved to the east coast of the US. Fall and winter continue to be my favorite seasons now! While I mostly study brains, I ocassionaly fly planes, and my love for nature and adventure never wanes :) So outside the lab, you can find me hiking, skiing, climbing, biking, running and doing most things outdoors!
